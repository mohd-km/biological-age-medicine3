Biological Age & Medicine 3.0 Survival Model

A routine blood test says more about how fast you are ageing than your birthday does. Move the sliders to see your biological age, which results move it most, and what a few small changes would do.

For learning only: a published research formula, not a medical device or a diagnosis. Talk to your doctor about your own results.

The problemTwo people can both be 45 and still be ageing at very different speeds. A birthday cannot show the difference. A routine blood test can.

How it worksPhenoAge is a published formula (Levine et al., 2018). It turns 9 routine blood results plus your age into a "biological age", which is the age at which an average person carries the same risk of dying in the next 10 years as you do. Model card: a Gompertz survival model (the risk of dying rises exponentially with age), fitted on a large US health survey. I checked it on 28,316 US adults followed for a median of 13 years, and it picked who died sooner better than age alone (details below).

Try this firstClick "Elevated Metabolic Risk" just below. The age stays at 45, yet biological age jumps from 43.1 to 62.4 and the 10-year risk of dying goes from 2.50% to 13.48%.

In plain words: (1) each blood result is multiplied by a weight and added up into a risk score, xb. (2) The Gompertz pattern, where an adult's risk of dying rises exponentially with age (in this model about 8% per year, so it roughly doubles every 8.6 years), turns that score into a risk of dying within 10 years, M. (3) That risk is turned back into the age at which an average person has the same risk. That age is your PhenoAge.

Where the model comes from: all ten PhenoAge Gompertz coefficients were fitted by Levine et al. (2018, Aging 10(4):573-591, doi:10.18632/aging.101414) on NHANES III/IV. This page runs their published equations unchanged, and automated unit tests check that.

1. Risk score (linear predictor, xb)
xb = -19.9067
   - 0.0336 * Albumin (g/L)
   + 0.0095 * Creatinine (umol/L)
   + 0.1953 * Glucose (mmol/L)
   + 0.0954 * ln(CRP in mg/dL)
   - 0.0120 * Lymphocyte (%)
   + 0.0268 * MCV (fL)
   + 0.3306 * RDW (%)
   + 0.00188 * ALP (U/L)
   + 0.0554 * WBC (10^3/uL)
   + 0.0804 * Age (years)

Why "normal" is not the same as "target": a lab's normal range covers the middle 95% of people, and many of them are already ageing badly. Inside that range, small rises in blood sugar, RDW, CRP and white cells still multiply risk years before any disease is diagnosed. Hence the targets here follow the prevention-first approach sometimes called Medicine 3.0.

The analysis code and its results

Every number here comes from the public CDC NHANES lab files and the national death-record linkage (NCHS 2019). One short Python script produces all of it, and anyone can rerun it.

  • analysis/nhanes_phenoage_validation.py: computes the exact ranking score (Harrell's C-index) from the CDC survey files and National Center for Health Statistics death records
  • src/data/nhanes_validation_results.json: the script's output, read by this page at build time
  • The formula on this page matches the analysis script to 9 decimal places, checked by automated tests
